The Trojan Horse is one of the most enduring tales from Greek mythology and the legendary ten-year Trojan War. According to tradition, the Greeks built a giant wooden horse and hid soldiers inside, pretending to abandon their siege of the city of Troy. The Trojans, believing the horse was a gift and a symbol of surrender, dragged it inside their walls as a trophy. Under the cover of night, the Greek soldiers emerged from the horse and opened the city gates to their returning army. The fall of Troy marked a decisive end to the war and became a symbol of deception and strategic cunning. Even today, the phrase 'Trojan horse' describes any trick that hides a threat inside something appearing harmless.
