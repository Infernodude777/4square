Dinosaurs were a diverse group of reptiles that dominated terrestrial ecosystems for over 140 million years during the Mesozoic Era. They first appeared around 230 million years ago during the Triassic period, evolving from earlier archosaur ancestors. The dinosaur lineage split into two main groups: the lizard-hipped Saurischia and the bird-hipped Ornithischia. While most dinosaurs went extinct at the end of the Cretaceous period about 66 million years ago, one lineage survived and evolved into modern birds. The largest known dinosaurs, the sauropods like Argentinosaurus, could reach lengths of over 100 feet and weigh as much as 100 tons. Today, paleontologists continue to discover new species at an impressive rate, with a new dinosaur named approximately every two weeks.
