Dinosaurs were a diverse group of reptiles that dominated Earth for over 160 million years, from the Triassic through the Jurassic and into the Cretaceous period. They ranged from tiny chicken-sized creatures to the enormous long-necked Argentinosaurus, filling nearly every ecological niche on land. Paleontologists continue to discover new species, using fossils to piece together how these ancient animals lived, moved, and interacted. Birds are now considered the direct descendants of theropod dinosaurs, meaning dinosaurs are not truly extinct. The most famous extinction event, caused by an asteroid impact, wiped out the non-avian dinosaurs around 66 million years ago. Today, dinosaur fossils inspire wonder in museums and movies around the world.
